What is 4D printing?

4D printing is 3D printing of smart materials that change shape over time. A part is printed from a stimulus-responsive material, usually a shape memory polymer, then "programmed" by heating, deforming and cooling it. Later, when the part meets a trigger such as heat, water or light, it moves into a new shape on its own. Time is the fourth dimension.

Because the shape change is built into the material, 4D printing can make self-folding structures, grippers and actuators without motors, deployable medical devices and adaptive metamaterials.

How 4D printing research started in India

In 2016, as an M.Tech student in Manufacturing Systems Engineering at Veer Surendra Sai University of Technology (VSSUT), Burla, Bijaya Bikram Samal began printing shape memory polymers on fused deposition modelling (FDM) printers and programming them to change shape in a controlled way. A year earlier, as a B.Tech student, he had built an FDM 3D printer from scratch.

In 2017 he presented this work with A. Jena and D. Mishra at COPEN 10, IIT Madras: Controlled Shape Changing Components by Using 4D Printing Technology. It is recorded as the first research in India on 4D printing.

In 2018, the international magazine Manufactur3D reported that Indian researchers had developed the world's strongest 4D-printed parts. The same year his M.Tech thesis, Controlled Shape Changing Components using 4D Printing, earned the University Gold Medal.

A Ph.D. dedicated to 4D printing

From 2018 he pursued a Ph.D. on the subject at IIT Kharagpur with an MHRD Doctoral Fellowship. His thesis, Four-Dimensional Printing: Shape Programming, Material Characterization, and Parametric Optimization, was awarded on 30 December 2024. Papers from this work appear in Smart Materials and Structures, the Journal of Mechanical Science and Technology and Materials Today: Proceedings, and he presented it in Germany, the USA, South Korea and the Netherlands.

A Wiley reference book on 4D printing

In 2025 Wiley-Scrivener published 4D Printing Technology: Principles, Materials and Application, with Dr. Samal as lead editor and author of six chapters, from the history of 3D-to-4D printing to characterization techniques and photonics applications.

4D printing research today

At IIT Kanpur, Dr. Samal now works on 4D-printed bioinspired Bouligand-auxetic metamaterials, multi-material direct ink writing, and a 4D-printed mechanical steganography device developed in consultation with the Indian Army. He also teaches 3D/4D printing workshops across India, from NIT Tiruchirappalli to NERIST in Arunachal Pradesh.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who started 4D printing research in India?

Dr. Bijaya Bikram Samal started 4D printing research in India in 2016, as an M.Tech student at Veer Surendra Sai University of Technology, Burla. His paper "Controlled Shape Changing Components by Using 4D Printing Technology", presented at COPEN 10, IIT Madras, in 2017, is recorded as the first research in India on 4D printing.

What is 4D printing?

4D printing is 3D printing of smart materials, such as shape memory polymers, that are programmed to change shape, stiffness or function over time when exposed to a stimulus like heat, water or light. Time is the fourth dimension.

Where is 4D printing research done in India?

Dr. Samal's 4D printing work began at VSSUT Burla (2016–2018), continued through his Ph.D. on four-dimensional printing at IIT Kharagpur (awarded 2024), and now continues at IIT Kanpur, where he works on 4D-printed bioinspired Bouligand-auxetic metamaterials.

Is there a book on 4D printing by an Indian author?

Yes. Dr. Bijaya Bikram Samal is the lead editor of "4D Printing Technology: Principles, Materials and Application" (Wiley-Scrivener, 2025), part of the Advances in Additive Manufacturing series.

What are the applications of 4D printing?

Applications include self-folding and deployable structures, soft robotic actuators, biomedical devices, photonics, smart textiles, and adaptive metamaterials that absorb impact or isolate vibration.
